# Hardy

**Citations** (as written in the doc comments):
- Hardy, Wright, An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers, 6th ed., Oxford University Press, 2008, Theorem 2 (the fundamental theorem of arithmetic: factorisation into primes is unique; stated in §1.3, proved in §2.10), so every divisor of p^k is p^j, j ≤ k
- Hardy, Wright, An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers, 6th ed., Oxford University Press, 2008, §20.5, Theorem 369 (Lagrange: every positive integer is a sum of four squares) and §20.10 (numbers 8m + 7 are not sums of three squares, so g(2) = 4)
- Hardy, Wright, An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers, 6th ed., Oxford University Press, 2008, §9.2 "Terminating and recurring decimals" (a rational number has a terminating or recurring decimal; the period follows the order of 10 modulo the denominator)
